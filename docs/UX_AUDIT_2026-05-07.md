**Date:** 2026-05-07

**Scope:** E2E audit of all public pages + landing page conversion analysis  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**5 Key Findings:**

1. **Broken resolution source links on 4/10 question detail pages** — URLs contain spaces, missing TLDs, or unresolvable patterns (e.g., `https://coingecko`, `https://ice / bloomberg`, `https://reuters / un security council`, `https://nature / science / doe`). This erodes trust and signals incomplete data quality.

2. **Landing page is content-rich but conversion-weak** — The page reads like a long-form essay (~2,000+ words of prose before the first CTA opportunity). Value proposition is buried in philosophical storytelling. A visitor cannot answer "what is this and what do I do?" in 5 seconds.

3. **No social proof or credibility signals** — OG meta description claims "Join thousands of forecasters" but the platform has 1 user and 1 forecast. Live stats show "2 Forecasters / 1 Predictions made" which feels empty. No testimonials, logos, press mentions, or user avatars exist anywhere.

4. **The "Start forecasting" CTA sends unauthenticated users to /questions, not /auth/signup** — This creates a 3-click funnel (landing → questions → question detail → "Log in") instead of a direct signup path. The primary CTA should route to signup.

5. **Missing OG image (og.png returns 404)** — Social sharing previews will show no image, reducing click-through rates from Twitter, Slack, Discord, etc.

## 3. Landing Page Conversion Analysis

### 3.1 Value Proposition Clarity (5-Second Test)

**Verdict: FAIL** ❌

A first-time visitor sees:
- "Beta — Free Play Mode" badge (confusing — what does "free play mode" mean?)
- "A different kind of intelligence" headline (evocative but uninformative)
- Two paragraphs of philosophical prose about probabilistic thinking

**What's missing in the first 5 seconds:**
- What the product IS (a prediction polling platform)
- What the user DOES (forecasts outcomes, gets scored)
- WHY they should care (test your judgment, compete, build a track record)

**Recommendation:** Replace or supplement the hero with a clear value proposition:
> "Predict real-world events. Get scored on accuracy. See how you stack up against the crowd."

### 3.2 CTA Analysis

| CTA | Location | Destination | Assessment |
|-----|----------|-------------|------------|
| "Start forecasting" | Hero section (primary position) | `/questions` | 🔴 **Wrong destination** — sends unauthenticated users to a dead end. Should go to `/auth/signup` |
| "Join the experiment" | Hero section (secondary) | `/auth/signup` | ✅ Correct, but "experiment" language is off-putting for mainstream users |
| "Experience it live" | Mid-page (after How It Works) | `/questions` | ⚠️ Same issue as primary CTA |
| "Create your account" | Bottom section | `/auth/signup` | ✅ Correct |
| "Get Started" | Nav bar | `/auth/signup` | ✅ Correct |

**Critical fix:** Swap the primary CTA destination from `/questions` → `/auth/signup`. The current flow forces 3 clicks before a user can sign up.

**CTA copy recommendations:**
- "Start forecasting" → "Start predicting — it's free"
- "Join the experiment" → "Create free account"
- Remove "experiment" framing — it signals instability and lack of commitment

### 3.3 Social Proof Analysis

**Current state: EMPTY** 🔴

The platform has:
- ❌ No user testimonials or quotes
- ❌ No press mentions or media logos
- ❌ No user count badges (and the "2 Forecasters" stat hurts more than it helps)
- ❌ No partner or advisor logos
- ❌ No community links (Discord, Twitter, etc.)

The only "social proof" is the stat line "10 Active questions / 2 Forecasters / 1 Predictions made" — which actually *undermines* credibility by showing how empty the platform is.

**Recommendations:**
1. **Remove or reframe the live stats** — Instead of "2 Forecasters", use "10 questions live" only, or remove entirely until meaningful traction exists
2. **Add a "Trusted by" or "Built for" section** — Reference the forecasting community (Good Judgment Project, IARPA, Philip Tetlock's Superforecasting)
3. **Add community links** — Discord server, Twitter/X account, newsletter signup
4. **Seed 3-5 testimonials** — Even from the founder/team about why prediction polling matters

### 3.4 Content Structure Analysis

**Current page structure (top to bottom):**
1. Nav (BAYCAST, Home, Questions, Blocks, Leaderboard, Get Started)
2. Beta badge
3. Hero: headline + 2 paragraphs of prose + 2 CTAs
4. Stats bar
5. "The crowd is wiser" — history lesson (Francis Galton, IARPA)
6. How It Works — 4 steps (numbered 01-04)
7. "Why prediction markets failed" — competitive positioning
8. 3 feature cards (scoring, AI agents, reputation)
9. "The Blind Consensus Protocol" — deep technical explanation
10. Blockquote + explanation
11. "Live questions" — 5 sample questions
12. "The crowd is waiting" — closing CTA section
13. Footer

**Problems:**
- **Too much prose before value clarity** — Sections 5-9 are educational content that belongs in a /about or /how-it-works page, not the landing page
- **No visual diversity** — Page is entirely text. No illustrations, diagrams, product screenshots, or demo videos
- **No above-the-fold product preview** — User can't see what the forecasting interface looks like
- **Feature section reads like a research paper** — "The Blind Consensus Protocol" section is 200+ words of academic framing

**Recommended landing page structure (conversion-optimized):**
1. Nav
2. **Hero**: Clear headline + subheadline + primary CTA + product screenshot/GIF
3. **Social proof bar**: Logos, stats, or community size
4. **How it works**: 3-4 steps (keep current, it's good) with visuals
5. **Live questions**: Move UP — show the product in action
6. **Why Baycast**: Competitive differentiation (condense current sections 7-9 into 3 punchy cards)
7. **Testimonials / community**
8. **Final CTA**: Strong closing with signup
