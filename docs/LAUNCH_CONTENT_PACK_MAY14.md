**Post body:**

```text
I built Baycast, an open-source prediction polling platform for forecasting real-world questions without money, trading, or gambling.

The core idea is simple: on most prediction platforms, you see the crowd estimate before you make your own forecast. That creates anchoring and herding. Baycast uses a Blind Consensus flow instead:

1. You see a question with clear resolution criteria
2. You submit your probability estimate without seeing the crowd
3. After you commit, the aggregate is revealed
4. You can update after seeing the consensus
5. Blind and revised forecasts are both scored

So the goal is not "trade better than other people." The goal is to measure judgment better.

Why this approach:
- Prediction polling, not gambling: no money, no crypto, no betting mechanics
- Blind Consensus preserves independent judgment before social influence kicks in
- Humans and AI can forecast the same questions under the same scoring rules
- It is free to use, so participation is not filtered by capital or jurisdiction

I think this creates a cleaner collective-intelligence system than a prediction market if what you care about is calibration and accuracy rather than liquidity.

Current honest status: very early. Baycast has 1 user, 10 live questions, 0 signups, and 251+ questions in the pipeline. I'm posting now because I want feedback on the method, the UX, and whether the human-vs-AI forecasting angle is interesting to people here.

If HN wants to stress-test it, I'd especially love feedback on:
- whether Blind Consensus is the right default interaction
- whether scoring blind vs revised forecasts separately is useful
- what kinds of questions are most worth forecasting this way

Live demo: https://baycast-p.vercel.app
Source: https://github.com/Smildous/baycast

Happy to answer questions about the protocol, scoring, or implementation.
```

Hi r/Superforecasters,

I built Baycast and wanted to share it here because the product thesis is closer to forecasting methodology than to prediction-market trading.

Baycast is a free, open-source prediction polling platform. The key idea is that you make your forecast before seeing the crowd estimate.

That matters because most forecasting products show the consensus, market price, or median up front. Once you see that number, your own estimate is no longer fully independent.

Baycast uses a Blind Consensus flow:
1. Read the question and resolution criteria
2. Submit a probability estimate without seeing the crowd
3. Reveal the aggregate only after commitment
4. Optionally revise after seeing the consensus
5. Score blind and revised forecasts separately

So the product is trying to measure two different skills:
- how good your raw judgment is before social influence
- how well you update after learning from the crowd

A few things that may be relevant to this sub:
- this is prediction polling, not gambling
- there is no money, crypto, or trading layer
- the platform is free to use
- humans and AI forecast the same questions under the same rules

That last point is especially interesting to me. Baycast can become a practical benchmark for human vs AI forecasting performance on identical real-world questions, instead of comparing them in completely different environments.
