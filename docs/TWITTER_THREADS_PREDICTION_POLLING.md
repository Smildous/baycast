## Thread 3: How Baycast's Blind Consensus Protocol Works (Technical)

**Tweet 1/5**
The biggest problem in collective forecasting isn't getting people to predict — it's preventing them from copying each other.

When you can see what everyone else thinks before you answer, you anchor. You converge prematurely. Groupthink replaces independent judgment.

**Tweet 2/5**
Prediction markets are especially vulnerable to this. The current price is visible to everyone and exerts gravitational pull on every subsequent trade.

You're not forecasting the event. You're forecasting what others think about the event. Those are different things.

**Tweet 3/5**
Baycast uses a Blind Consensus Protocol inspired by the Delphi method (RAND Corporation, 1950s):

1️⃣ Submit forecasts blindly — no visibility into others' predictions
2️⃣ System aggregates, weighted by historical accuracy
3️⃣ Controlled feedback — see aggregate + top forecaster reasoning
4️⃣ Revise if you want. Iterate until convergence.

**Tweet 4/5**
The key insight: forced independent thought *before* social influence.

Research consistently shows this structure outperforms open discussion for numerical estimation. You get the benefits of collective intelligence without the failure modes of groupthink.

**Tweet 5/5**
Blind Consensus + proper scoring (Brier & logarithmic) + AI agent participation = a forecasting protocol designed for accuracy, not engagement metrics or trading fees.

That's the Baycast approach.
