## Banned claims

Do not say or imply:

- Baycast has traction, momentum, adoption, a community, or meaningful volume.
- The first score proves accuracy, quality, product market fit, or demand.
- Baycast predicts the future better than experts, markets, polls, or AI.
- Baycast is a betting site, prediction market, trading product, odds board, or gambling product.
- People can win money, earn yield, place bets, trade positions, or profit from forecasts.
- Any exact forecast count, user count, daily activity number, conversion number, or growth rate.
- The crowd was right unless the scored page clearly supports that exact wording.
- The system is calibrated based on one score.
- Public scoring means the product is mature, proven, or ready for broad launch.

Safe frame: Baycast is prediction polling. People answer questions with probabilities, then public outcomes turn those forecasts into a score. The goal is better forecasting habits and scored collective intelligence, not betting.

## Short founder note

Today Baycast got its first public score.

That sounds small because it is small. It also matters.

A prediction poll is only useful if it eventually faces reality. A question gets asked, people give probabilities, the outcome arrives, and the score becomes visible. That loop is now public.

Baycast is not betting. There are no markets, trades, or payouts. It is a place to turn opinions into forecasts and let those forecasts be scored when the real world catches up.

This first score is not a victory lap. It is the start of the part that matters: calibration, cleaner questions, and learning how a group gets less wrong over time.
