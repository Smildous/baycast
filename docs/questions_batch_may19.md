## Questions

### Tech

1. **Will Microsoft announce cumulative AI infrastructure spending of $100 billion or more for fiscal year 2026 (July 2025–June 2026)?**
   - *Est. probability: 40%* — Microsoft has committed massive capital expenditure to AI data centers. In FY2025, the company guided to ~$80B in capex, heavily weighted toward AI infrastructure. A jump to $100B+ would represent a 25%+ increase.
   - End date: 2026-08-31
   - Resolution: YES if Microsoft reports (in its FY2026 annual report, 10-K filing, or an official earnings call) that total capital expenditure for FY2026 was $100 billion or more. If Microsoft reports only "AI infrastructure spending" as a subcomponent exceeding $100B while total capex is lower, this resolves as YES only if the stated AI infrastructure figure exceeds $100B. If Microsoft does not break out AI infrastructure separately, total capex is used.
   - Source: https://microsoft.com/investor; https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?company=microsoft&CIK=789019

2. **Will the European Commission issue a fine of €5 billion or more to a single company for violating the Digital Services Act (DSA) before September 30, 2026?**
   - *Est. probability: 25%* — The DSA allows fines of up to 6% of global turnover. The EC has opened investigations into several major platforms (X/Twitter, TikTok, AliExpress). A €5B+ fine would signal aggressive enforcement, requiring a very large company to be found in violation.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if the European Commission officially announces a DSA fine (adopted decision under Article 74 DSA) of €5,000,000,000 or more against a single company. The fine must be formally adopted by the Commission, not merely proposed. Fines under other legislation (GDPR, DMA, Digital Markets Act) do not count — only DSA-specific fines. If multiple companies are fined simultaneously, each must be individually ≥€5B for this to count as YES.
   - Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act-package; https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/documents

3. **Will a Level 4 autonomous vehicle service be commercially available to the general public in at least 3 US cities before September 30, 2026?**
   - *Est. probability: 35%* — Waymo operates in several cities (Phoenix, SF, LA, Austin) but at varying levels of public access. Cruise and others are working toward expansion. "Commercially available" means any member of the public can request a ride without being on a waitlist.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if at least one company operating Level 4 autonomous vehicles (no human safety driver) offers a commercial ride-hailing service available to the general public (no waitlist, no invite-only restriction) in at least 3 distinct US cities simultaneously. The cities must be within the continental US. Each city must have the service actively accepting ride requests from any member of the public. The service must use SAE Level 4 automation. Airport shuttle services, fixed-route services, or delivery-only services do not count — must be on-demand ride-hailing.
   - Source: https://waymo.com; https://www.nhtsa.gov; https://www.transportation.gov

### Geopolitics

   - *Est. probability: 30%* — The 2026 G20 summit is expected in November 2026 in India (rotating presidency). However, a joint statement could come earlier. Given divergent US, EU, Chinese, and Indian AI regulatory approaches, a binding commitment is ambitious.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if G20 leaders issue an official joint communiqué, declaration, or statement (published on the G20 official website or the hosting country's government site) that includes a commitment described as "binding," "legally binding," "mandatory," or equivalent language regarding AI governance, AI safety standards, or AI development rules. Voluntary commitments, principles, guidelines, or "we encourage" language without binding force resolves as NO. If the 2026 G20 summit has not occurred by September 30, any prior G20 ministerial or working group statement with binding AI commitments counts.
   - Source: https://www.g20.org; https://www.mea.gov.in (if India hosts)

The document must be publicly available or officially announced by both parties. Informal talks, prisoner exchanges, or local truces without a signed agreement do not count. If the Fano militia is dissolved or defeated militarily without a signed agreement, this resolves as NO.
   - Source: https://mfa.gov.et; https://reuters.com; https://apnews.com

6. **Will South Korea and Japan sign a new bilateral security or intelligence-sharing agreement before September 30, 2026?**

### Economics

7. **Will Germany enter a technical recession (two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth) at any point before September 30, 2026?**
   - *Est. probability: 40%* — Germany's economy has been sluggish, hampered by energy costs, weak Chinese demand for exports, and structural challenges in manufacturing. The risk of recession is elevated but not certain.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if Destatis (the German Federal Statistical Office) reports negative quarter-over-quarter real GDP growth for two consecutive quarters, with the second quarter ending on or before June 30, 2026 (meaning Q4 2025/Q1 2026 or Q1 2026/Q2 2026). The growth figures used are the Destatis seasonally and calendar-adjusted preliminary estimates (first publication). If Destatis revises a previously positive quarter to negative, the revised figure is used. Exactly 0.0% growth resolves as NO.
   - Source: https://www.destatis.de/EN/Themes/Economy/National-Accounts

8. **Will the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) cut the cash rate at any board meeting before September 30, 2026?**
   - *Est. probability: 45%* — The RBA has been one of the last major central banks to begin cutting rates. Inflation has been sticky in Australia. A cut would signal the RBA is satisfied that inflation is returning to the 2-3% target band.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if the RBA board announces a reduction in the cash rate target at any scheduled or emergency board meeting before September 30, 2026. A cut of any size (25bps, 15bps, etc.) counts. Maintaining or raising the rate resolves as NO. The announcement must come via the official RBA monetary policy decision statement. If no RBA meeting occurs (extremely unlikely), resolves as NO.
   - Source: https://www.rba.gov.au/monetary-policy

### Science

9. **Will ESA's JUICE spacecraft successfully enter orbit around Jupiter before September 30, 2026?**
   - *Est. probability: 30%* — ESA's Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer (JUICE) launched in April 2023 and is scheduled to arrive at Jupiter in July 2031. However, the question asks about orbital entry, which is not expected until 2031. This is a long-shot question testing whether the mission timeline is accelerated.
   - End date: 2026-09-30
   - Resolution: YES if ESA officially confirms that the JUICE spacecraft has successfully performed its Jupiter Orbit Insertion (JOI) maneuver and is in stable orbit around Jupiter before September 30, 2026. Given the planned 2031 arrival, this is intentionally a low-probability question in case of a trajectory shortcut or mission reprogramming. Gravity assists without Jupiter orbit insertion do not count.
   - Source: https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Juice; https://sci.esa.int

10. **Will the September 2026 Arctic sea ice minimum extent fall below 3.5 million square kilometers?**
    - *Est. probability: 45%* — Arctic sea ice has been declining over decades. The record minimum was 3.39 million sq km in 2012. Recent years have generally ranged between 4.0-4.5 million sq km. A very warm summer could push the minimum below 3.5M.
    - End date: 2026-10-15
    - Resolution: YES if the National Snow and Ice Data Center (NSIDC) reports that the 2026 Arctic sea ice minimum extent (typically reached in mid-September) is below 3.50 million square kilometers in their official monthly summary. NSIDC's "Sea Ice Index" daily extent data is the authoritative source. The minimum is defined as the lowest daily extent value recorded during September 2026. If NSIDC has not published the September 2026 summary by October 15, resolution extends to November 1.
    - Source: https://nsidc.org/arcticseaicenews; https://nsidc.org/data/seaice_index

### Sport

11. **Will the 2026 FIFA World Cup be completed without a single red card being shown in any match?**
    - *Est. probability: 20%* — This is intentionally a low-probability question. With 64 matches in the tournament, the probability of zero red cards is very low. The 2018 World Cup had 3 red cards; 2022 had 4. A red-card-free World Cup would be extremely unusual.
    - End date: 2026-07-19
    - Resolution: YES if no player receives a red card (straight red or second yellow resulting in dismissal) in any of the 64 matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup (June 11 – July 19), as recorded in the official FIFA match reports. Red cards shown after the final whistle, to coaches/staff (not players), or in abandoned matches that are later replayed do not count. If a red card is rescinded on appeal after the tournament, the original on-field decision stands for resolution purposes (the question is about whether a red card was shown, not whether it was upheld).
    - Source: https://www.fifa.com/fifaplus/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/2026

12. **Will a first-time Tour de France winner take the 2026 general classification?**
    - *Est. probability: 40%* — The Tour de France has seen new winners emerge periodically (Pogačar won his first in 2020, Vingegaard in 2022). With Tadej Pogačar and Jonas Vingegaard as the dominant duo, a first-time winner would require one of them to not win. The field includes potential challengers like Evenepoel, Ayuso, and others who haven't previously won the Tour.
    - End date: 2026-07-27
    - Resolution: YES if the rider who wins the 2026 Tour de France general classification (yellow jersey) has never previously won the Tour de France in any year. A rider who has won the Giro d'Italia or Vuelta a España but not the Tour de France counts as a first-time Tour winner. If the winner is later disqualified and the yellow jersey is reassigned, the final official winner (after all appeals) is used for resolution. If the Tour is cancelled, resolves as NO.
    - Source: https://www.letour.fr/en; https://www.procyclingstats.com
