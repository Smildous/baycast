# Why I Built Baycast: Prediction Polls for Everyone

I've always been obsessed with the future. Not in a crystal-ball way — more like: what do we *actually* know about what's going to happen? And who should we trust to tell us?

A few years ago, I stumbled into the world of prediction markets and forecasting tournaments. Polymarket was blowing up. Metaculus had its cult following. Kalshi was fighting the SEC. It felt like the beginning of something important — collective intelligence, crowdsourced truth, the wisdom of the crowd made digital.

But the more I used these platforms, the more something bugged me.

**Prediction markets are broken for normal people.**

Polymarket is exciting, sure. But it requires real money. You need crypto. You need to be comfortable gambling. And the "wisdom" it surfaces isn't really wisdom — it's wealth. A whale with $500K moves the price more than 500 people who happen to be excellent forecasters. That's not the wisdom of crowds. That's the wisdom of capital.

Metaculus is brilliant, don't get me wrong. But it's built for a specific kind of person — policy wonks, academic researchers, people who think in Brier scores the way the rest of us think in dollars. The barrier to entry isn't financial; it's cultural. If you're not already deep in the forecasting community, it can feel like walking into a PhD seminar halfway through the semester.

And Kalshi? Regulated to death. Literally fighting court battles just to exist. By the time they got approval to list election contracts, the constraints were so tight that the product felt neutered. Innovation strangled by compliance.

So I started asking: what if there were a platform that captured the best parts of all three — the excitement of Polymarket, the rigor of Metaculus, the legitimacy of Kalshi — without any of the downsides?

**That's Baycast.**

Baycast is a prediction polling platform. Not a prediction market. Not gambling. Not a research tool for think tanks. Prediction *polls* — free, scored, social questions about the future that anyone can answer.

Here's how it works: we ask questions about real-world outcomes. Will AI top the Billboard charts before 2028? Will Bitcoin hit $200K? Will a country adopt AI-written legislation? You give us a probability — "I think there's a 35% chance" — and when the outcome resolves, we score you.

Not on how much money you risked. On how *accurate* you were.

We use Brier scoring and logarithmic scoring, which is a fancy way of saying: if you say "70% chance" a hundred times, we check whether it actually happened about 70 times. That's calibration. And calibration is the currency that matters.

We also built something called the Blind Consensus Protocol. It's inspired by the Delphi method — a technique RAND Corporation developed in the 1950s. The idea is simple: if you can see what everyone else is predicting before you answer, you'll anchor on the crowd. You'll stop thinking independently. So instead, we collect forecasts *blindly*, aggregate them, and then share the results. You think for yourself first. Then you see the group's answer. Then — if you want — you can revise.

Independence before influence. It produces better forecasts. It's been proven for 70 years. We just made it easy and social.

The vision behind Baycast is bigger than a single platform. I believe — and the research backs this up — that a diverse crowd of everyday forecasters will outperform a small group of wealthy traders every time. Philip Tetlock's superforecasting research showed that the best predictors aren't experts, aren't credentialed, and aren't rich. They're just people who think in probabilities, update their beliefs, and practice.

But those people need a place to practice. A place that doesn't charge them. A place that doesn't require a crypto wallet or a statistics degree. A place that meets them where they are and rewards them for being right.

That's the Baycast I want to build. Free prediction polls for everyone. Scored. Social. No gatekeeping. No tokens. No gambling.

Just your judgment, tracked over time, compared against the world.
