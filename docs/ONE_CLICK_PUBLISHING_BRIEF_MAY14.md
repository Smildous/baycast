```markdown
Most prediction products have the same hidden flaw: they show you the crowd's answer before you make your own prediction.

That feels helpful. It is also a source of anchoring.

If you see that a market says 73%, or a platform median says 62%, your estimate is no longer fully independent. You may still think carefully, but the number is already in your head. The forecast becomes partly your judgment and partly your reaction to the crowd.

I built Baycast to test a different default.

Baycast is a free, open-source prediction polling platform. It is not a prediction market. There is no money, no crypto, no trading, and no gambling mechanic. You forecast real-world questions with probabilities, then get scored on accuracy.

The core mechanic is called Blind Consensus.

1. You read a question and its resolution criteria.
2. You submit your probability estimate before seeing what anyone else thinks.
3. After you commit, the aggregate forecast is revealed.
4. You can optionally revise after seeing the consensus.
5. Your blind forecast and revised forecast can both be evaluated.

That gives the platform two useful signals:

- independent judgment before social influence
- update quality after seeing the crowd

I think that distinction matters. Some people are strong original forecasters but bad updaters. Others start rough but become excellent after seeing the distribution of views. Most products collapse those two skills into one number.

Baycast also makes it possible for humans and AI systems to forecast the same questions under the same protocol. Same question. Same blind phase. Same scoring. That could become a practical benchmark for human-vs-AI forecasting performance on real-world questions.
```

## 3-reply response bank

Use these as starting points. Personalize the first sentence if needed, but keep the substance.

### Reply 1 — When someone asks “How is this different from prediction markets?”

```text
The main difference is the optimization target.

Prediction markets aggregate capital: the signal is expressed through money, risk tolerance, liquidity, and sometimes strategic trading.

Baycast is trying to aggregate judgment directly. No money, no trading, no crypto. You submit a probability, initially without seeing the crowd, and the forecast is scored against the eventual outcome.

So I don't think of it as a market replacement for people who want to trade. I think of it as a forecasting gym for people who want to measure calibration and improve judgment.
```

### Reply 2 — When someone challenges Blind Consensus / says seeing the crowd is useful

```text
I agree that seeing the crowd is useful. The question is when.

Baycast does not hide the crowd forever. It hides it before your first forecast so the system can capture an independent estimate. After you commit, the consensus is revealed and you can revise.

That creates two data points: your raw judgment and your update after social information. I think both are valuable, and most platforms only capture the second one because the crowd estimate is visible from the start.
```

### Reply 3 — When someone points out “0 signups” or says it is too early

```text
Totally fair. It is very early.

I'm sharing it now precisely because the product needs real forecasters poking holes in the method and UX before I pretend it has traction. The current status is 1 user, 10 live questions, 0 signups, and 251+ questions in the pipeline.

If the core mechanic is wrong, I want to learn that now. If it is right but confusing, I want to fix that before scaling it.
```
