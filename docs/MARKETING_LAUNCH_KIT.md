### First Comment

We built Baycast because we think prediction markets have a fundamental problem: they've become gambling platforms masquerading as forecasting tools.

When you put money on the line, the signal degrades. People bet on what they *want* to happen, not what they *think* will happen. Insider trading becomes profitable. Regulators crack down. We're just getting started with a small community of forecasters. If you've ever been curious about prediction markets but put off by the gambling aspect, give Baycast a try. Your first forecast takes 30 seconds.

### Description

Hey HN,

I built Baycast because I got frustrated with prediction markets. Every platform I tried — Polymarket, Kalshi, Manifold — has the same problem: money creates noise. When there's financial skin in the game, people stop forecasting and start gambling. You get insider trading (OpenAI just fired someone for it), regulatory crackdowns (the Senate literally banned itself from prediction markets), and a signal that's contaminated by wealth effects.

Baycast strips all of that away. It's a scored polling platform: you answer questions about real-world events (Will GPT-5 ship before July? Will the EU pass an AI Act amendment?), and we score your accuracy over time using Brier scores. No money on the line. No crypto. No gambling.

**Tech stack:** Next.js 14 (App Router), Supabase for auth + Postgres, deployed on Vercel. OG images generated server-side. The whole thing is open source.

**What makes it interesting to me:** The prediction accuracy of crowds is genuinely powerful when the incentives are aligned. Polymarket was remarkably accurate about the 2024 US election — but it was also a $3B gambling operation with all the problems that entails. I think you can get 80% of the signal with 0% of the toxicity by just... not making it about money.

We're early — small community, ~10 live questions, building in public. Would love feedback from the HN crowd, especially from anyone who's into forecasting, superforecasting, or decision science.

### Trend Analysis

**1. The "Gambling" Narrative Has Won the Mainstream**
The top HN posts of the past month are overwhelmingly negative about prediction markets. The Atlantic, The Verge, and even Warren Buffett are framing prediction markets as gambling — not forecasting. This is a **massive tailwind for Baycast's positioning**. The more toxic the "prediction market" brand becomes, the more our "scored polling, not gambling" message resonates.

**2. Regulatory Crackdown Is Accelerating**
- Federal: Senate self-ban, SEC ETF delays, Trump admin preemption attempts
- State: NY employee ban, Texas loophole closure, Minnesota guardrails, Arizona judicial fights
- The regulatory environment is getting worse for US-based prediction market operators. **Baycast's EU base is a structural advantage.**

**3. Insider Trading Is Now a Front-Page Issue**
OpenAI firing + Senate bans + U.S. soldier charged with using classified info = prediction markets are now seen as insider trading vehicles. **Baycast eliminates this entirely by removing financial incentives.**

**4. AI + Prediction Markets = Controversial**
The intersection of AI and prediction markets is hot (HBR article on AI worsening predictions, OpenAI scandal). **Baycast can position AI questions as forecasting topics rather than trading vehicles.**

**5. Market Entry: Too Many Gambling Platforms**
SnapMarkets, Hyperliquid targeting Kalshi/Polymarket, multiple HN Show HN projects doing Kalshi/Polymarket arbitrage. The "prediction market as gambling" space is **crowded and getting more crowded**. The "prediction polling without money" space is **nearly empty**.

### Implications for Baycast Positioning

1. **Double down on "not gambling"** — the market is screaming for a non-gambling alternative. This is no longer a niche position; it's becoming the mainstream preference.
2. **EU credibility** — with US regulation in chaos, being EU-based is a trust signal, not just a legal necessity.
3. **Accuracy over money** — the insider trading scandals prove that financial incentives corrupt the signal. Lead with "accurate forecasting" not "no money" (the latter is a feature of the former).
4. **Question quality matters** — with platforms like SnapMarkets going for speed/volume, Baycast should emphasize thoughtful, well-resolved questions.
5. **AI angle is hot** — prediction questions about AI (GPT-5, regulation, AGI timelines) are highly relevant and currently undercovered on non-gambling platforms.
